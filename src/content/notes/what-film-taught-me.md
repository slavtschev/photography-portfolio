You press the shutter thirty-six times and then you stop. That constraint is not a limitation — it's a form of attention.

When I moved to digital I thought I was gaining something. More images, more chances, more flexibility. And I was. But I was also losing something I hadn't noticed I had: the weight of each frame.

On film, every click costs something. Not much — a few pence, maybe a minute of development time — but enough to make you pause. Enough to make you ask whether you're photographing something or just pointing at it.

The images I've made on film are not always technically better. Sometimes they're softer, slightly off. But they almost always feel more considered. There's a quality to a photograph that knows it only had one chance.

I still shoot both. But film is where I go to slow down.
