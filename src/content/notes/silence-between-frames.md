Every photograph is surrounded by the photographs not taken — the moment before, the moment after, the angle I didn't choose.

I've been thinking about this lately: the frame is not a capture, it's a selection. And what you leave out is just as much a decision as what you include. The silence between frames is where the meaning actually lives.

When I'm editing a series, the hardest part is not choosing the best image — it's deciding how many to remove. A sequence of twelve tells a different story than a sequence of four. Four is a statement. Twelve is a conversation. Both can be right. But they are not the same thing.

I keep a folder called "the ones that almost made it." I look at it sometimes. Not to second-guess the edit, but to remember that the work was there.
