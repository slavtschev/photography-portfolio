Colour is not just descriptive. At its best, it does something — it creates a feeling that the image couldn't produce without it.

I worked in black and white for a long time because I was afraid of colour. It seemed too easy to get wrong. Too much going on. Too many decisions I didn't know how to make.

What changed was looking at Eggleston. Not for style, but for the way he treated colour as subject matter — not background. The red of a ceiling. The green of a parking lot. These are not incidental. They are what the photograph is about.

Since then I've tried to ask, with every coloured frame: what is this colour doing? Is it here to describe, or to mean something? If I can't answer that, I usually convert to monochrome.

Not because monochrome is safer — it isn't — but because a photograph should know what it is.
